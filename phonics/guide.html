What the course is

149 original lesson plans organised around the six-phase structure of the 2007 Letters and Sounds framework. Topics include listening awareness, early sound–spelling relationships, digraphs, adjacent consonants, alternative spellings, fluency and spelling strategies. This is not every lesson or activity from the published programme. Use the official source for the complete original publication.

The 44-tile atlas is an original arrangement with mnemonic captions and emoji memory anchors. It does not reproduce the THRASS chart or its teaching method. The graphics are not yet a commissioned custom illustration set.

Keep three audio jobs separate

  1. Word and instruction narration: available English device voices read lesson instructions, examples and whole chart-anchor words. Australian voices are prioritised, then UK voices; no Australian accent is promised when the device has none. Novelty voices are excluded. Voice quality varies, and a bare letter within an instruction may be spoken as its name. These controls do not provide isolated phoneme models.
  2. Isolated phoneme models: clean, teacher-reviewed sound recordings, without extra vowels or letter names. No clips are supplied. Teachers can now add their own permitted models using the Teacher desk library: choose a sound and accent, confirm rights/privacy consent, upload audio or explicitly choose Record, preview the whole clip, confirm human review and save. Capturing a recording alone never saves it.
  3. Learner speaking: voluntary practice and human feedback, not an automatic accent or ability score.

Never claim that hearing a browser read “s” gives a reliable pure /s/. Stop consonants should be brief; an added “uh” makes blending harder. A voiced/voiceless contrast and a letter-name contrast need different examples.

Use the voice and speed controls, then choose a labelled read-aloud button. Stop or navigate to end narration. Long passages are read in bounded chunks; there is no claimed word-timing alignment. Playback starts only after a click. No app API or paid provider is configured, but some operating-system voices can send speech text to their provider. A voice labelled local only reports what the browser exposes; this is not an independent privacy guarantee. If no suitable voice is available, read the text or ask a teacher. Browser support for pause/resume and playback varies; actual pronunciation and sound quality need human listening review.

Listen and point with supplied models

The Listen & point view loads reviewed models from this browser only. At least two models labelled Australian English or UK English are required; labels do not independently verify a recording’s actual accent. Other / not specified models remain available on Teacher desk but are excluded from automatic drills. No missing clip is replaced with TTS.

Start six untimed rounds, play the mystery clip to the end, then choose a sound tile. Replay keeps the same sound and choices. Incorrect choices allow retries; a revealed model is supported practice, not an independent success. Next unlocks after a correct answer or an explicit reveal. The summary keeps first-try accuracy separate from retries and reveals. Completed summaries may be stored as local practice history; they are not a validated phonics assessment, pronunciation grade or accent judgement. Client-side code contains the answer; there are no anti-cheating or secure gradebook claims.

Respect accent and language

The atlas uses a traditional UK reference inventory, not an accent checklist. Vowel categories and r pronunciation vary across UK, Australian and other English varieties. Ear, air and ure can merge or be realised differently. Use a consistent appropriate teacher model, invite learners to compare languages, and do not penalise a valid accent difference.

Listening vocabulary is not decoding text

Early Phase Two lessons separate teacher-supported listening vocabulary from reading words built with spellings introduced so far. The first two discovery lessons do not expect whole-word decoding. A mnemonic image can help remember a sound, but must not become a cue to guess an unfamiliar written word.

Privacy and progress

The current draft has no real accounts, shared classroom records or server recording uploads. Sound Studio learning history uses its own namespaced browser storage key, separate from the clip database and the original classroom demo. It records reflection attempt counts, immutable first answers, latest answers and explicitly self-reported aloud practice. Simply opening a lesson or playing narration does not mark it as practised or mastered. Completed listening summaries retain first-try matches, reveals and retries; only the latest 50 sessions are kept. A model revealed after a correct response can appear in both the first-try and reveal counts, so those counts are not necessarily disjoint. Do not enter personal details or sensitive student material. Local sound-model controls explain storage, microphone permission and deletion before use. Models are stored in a dedicated IndexedDB database on this browser and origin, separate from the classroom demo. Files are limited to 1 MB and decoded as 0.1–10 seconds. An existing model requires confirmation before replacement; Delete local model asks before removing that sound. Discard removes only the unsaved preview. Clearing browser data can remove saved models; there is no cloud backup or cross-device sharing. Changing the chosen sound/accent or withdrawing consent discards the current preview. Navigation/page exit cancels recording and releases preview resources. A save already submitted to storage may finish even if you leave the view. The original classroom demo and its separate storage are unchanged.

Use adult/teacher models only, with permission and no student personal information. Microphone access starts only after the explicit Record button and consent checkbox; deny or cancel if unsure. The app checks format/length and review steps, but cannot determine whether a clip is genuinely a correct pure phoneme or prove a person listened attentively. Use a trained teacher’s judgement. Isolated phonemes are not automatically generated from TTS, and no phoneme library has been professionally auditioned here.

Practice history is not a secure gradebook, verified speech evidence or cross-device backup. Storage failures are reported; transient state may last only in the current tab. Corrupt stored data is not silently replaced. Reset learning history asks for confirmation and removes only the Sound Studio history key, not saved sound models, classroom-demo data or school records. The same shared browser profile shares this history; there are no learner accounts or identity separation.

Accessibility and checks

The interface uses keyboard-operable native controls, visible focus, a skip link, descriptive sound-tile labels, non-colour labels, status announcements and a print layout. These are implemented features, not a claim of a completed accessibility audit. Print first restores all 44 tiles regardless of the active filter. Speech, recording, physical printer output and classroom suitability still need real-device review.
